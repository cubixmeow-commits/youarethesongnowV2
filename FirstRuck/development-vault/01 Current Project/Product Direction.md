# Product direction

## Purpose

FirstRuck helps someone new to rucking prepare safely, choose a manageable outing, go outside, record the experience, reflect, and want to return. The first useful payoff is a plan the person can actually do. The lasting value is a personal record of places, photos, progress, and confidence.

## Audience

The first audience is a beginner or returning walker who is curious about rucking but does not want military posturing, extreme challenges, guilt, or competitive fitness culture. They need clear preparation, conservative choices, local routes, and reassurance without being patronized.

## Product principles

- Make the first outing feel achievable and worthwhile.
- Alternate questions with useful rewards so onboarding never becomes an interrogation.
- Ask only for information that changes the plan, preparation, or route.
- Earn the membership screen by showing a personal plan and route first.
- Build consistency before load, distance, or hill progression.
- Change one training variable at a time.
- Treat turning back, resting, or choosing no added load as valid decisions.
- Make memories and exploration more prominent than calories, streaks, or leaderboards.
- Keep private activity private unless the user deliberately shares it.
- Use real geographic sources for facts and use AI only within verified boundaries.

## Differentiation

FirstRuck should feel like a living outdoor field guide rather than an AI-generated workout dashboard. Kip supplies continuity and warmth. Local route creation, gentle preparation, during-walk check-ins, photo memories, field-journal postcards, and a visible journey give the app emotional value beyond a timer.

## Experience shape

Invitation → questions and field notes → preparation → personal plan → route → readiness → membership → Today → record → reflect → journal → journey.

## Business hypothesis

People may pay for a continually adapting first-month plan, practical route help, recording, reflection, and a journal that becomes personally meaningful. Pricing, trial design, free-tier limits, and store products remain undecided and must be based on real products and tests.

## Success signals to define

- Onboarding completion and question-level drop-off
- Route selected after plan reveal
- First walk started and completed
- Second walk within a meaningful period without streak pressure
- Journal memory or postcard created
- Membership view and purchase conversion after real pricing exists
- Safety stops and location-permission declines without broken journeys
